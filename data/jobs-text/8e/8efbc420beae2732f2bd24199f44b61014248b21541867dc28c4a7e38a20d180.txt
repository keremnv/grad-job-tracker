WHAT THAT LOOKS LIKE DAY TO DAY

You're our first manager of Customer Success in the US, and stepping into a unique scenario: you are not coming in to fix something — the team is already strong, and our retention metrics are the gold standard. Your job is to take what's working and make it repeatable — forecasting, renewals, customer health, the rhythms that hold up when the team is twice the size.

You'll lead CSMs across New York and San Francisco, spanning white glove, high and medium touch motions. You'll report into our CRO until we hire a global VP of Customer Success — a role we're actively working on — which means for now you've got a direct line to commercial leadership and unusual latitude to shape how the function runs. You'll have a counterpart running CS in EMEA, and the two of you will set the standard globally.

Expect to be a player-coach. You'll run forecast reviews and 1:1s, and you'll also be on customer calls — sometimes because an account needs you, often because you want to be close enough to the work to know whether the playbook you're writing actually holds. You'll partner tightly with Sales, Product, Marketing and RevOps, and you'll be one of the clearest voices of the customer inside the company, connecting what you're hearing in the field to where the product goes next.

We're AI-forward in two directions here. Our customers buy an AI incident response product, so fluency in what it does and where it's going is part of the job. And internally, we expect you and your team to be using AI in the actual workflow — account research, health signals, call prep, QBR prep — and to push that further rather than wait for someone to hand you a process.
